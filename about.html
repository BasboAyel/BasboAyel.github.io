About / 01

A researcher who builds with people.

My work is driven by a simple question: how can computing make biological and assistive research more useful to the people it is meant to serve?

The work

Three fields, one thread.

My work sits at the intersection of computer science, biology, and human-centered design. I have studied gene expression in lung tissue models, prototyped assistive hardware for people with sensory disabilities, and built the software research teams use day to day.

The difficult problems in health and accessibility need people who can read a differential expression plot, write reliable code, and spend enough time with users to understand what actually matters. I am working to become that kind of researcher.

The path

01 / Ecology

Start with messy, real-world data.

Field surveys of Great Blue Heron rookeries and prairie lake microplastics contamination taught me to look for signal in complex environmental systems.

02 / Computational biology

Ask better biological questions.

At Yale School of Medicine, I analyze single-cell data from tissue-engineered lung bioreactor models.

03 / Accessibility & HCI

Design with—not just for—people.

At Madison Cyber Labs, I work on multisensory environments, assistive prototypes, and accessible technology evaluation.

04 / Software

Make research usable at scale.

From genomics tools to caregiver communication systems, I build the infrastructure that turns research into something people can use.
